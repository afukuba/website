About me

My name is Patrick O’Melveny (he/him). I’m a master’s student at San Francisco State University, currently applying to Ph.D. programs in mathematics for the upcoming 2026–2027 academic year.

My master’s thesis under Dusty Ross is a new proof of the log‑concavity of characteristic polynomials of matroids. I’m currently interested in the fields of combinatorics and algebraic geometry.

In my past lives, I’ve been a software engineer and a music teacher. I’d love opportunities to incorporate either into my pursuits in theoretical math — counterpoint and compilers both have a lot to say about structure.

№01

Mixed Volumes of Normal Complexes

with Lauren Nowak, Dusty Ross

We study mixed volumes of a class of simplicial complexes associated to matroids and show how they refine classical Hodge–Riemann style inequalities in combinatorial contexts.

№02

A Geometric Proof of the Log‑Concavity of Characteristic Polynomials of Matroids

solo · Master’s thesis, San Francisco State University

A new, largely geometric argument for log‑concavity of the characteristic polynomial of a (loopless) matroid, leaning on the combinatorial Hodge theory developed by Adiprasito, Huh, and Katz. Draft, updated occasionally.

The Euler Characteristic on 2‑Manifolds

with John Gallagher, Henry Guerrero‑Duarte · final project for Math 450: Topology

We introduce and prove the simple case of the Euler characteristic on orientable 2‑manifolds, with a short history and motivation vis‑à‑vis graph theory.

Game Theory Final Project: Rent Negotiation

with Montse Cordero · final project for Math 491: Game Theory

A real‑world application of the game‑theoretic techniques we’d learned so far. In the heart of San Francisco during the pandemic, we were inspired by the rare reversal of rents going on around us. It’s nice to know even math says it’s terrible to be a renter in the Bay Area.

≈ Manifesto

I believe in Federico Ardila’s axioms from Todos Cuentan:

01

Mathematical potential is distributed equally among different groups, irrespective of geographic, demographic, and economic boundaries.

02

Everyone can have joyful, meaningful, and empowering mathematical experiences.

03

Mathematics is a powerful, malleable tool that can be shaped and used differently by various communities to serve their needs.

04

Every student deserves to be treated with dignity and respect.
